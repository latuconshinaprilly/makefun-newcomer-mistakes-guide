A getting-started guide for [make.fun](https://make.fun/?ref=0ys1yw2z),
written as a list of the ten mistakes almost every newcomer makes in their
first week — and the small habit that replaces each one. Read it top to
bottom before your first entry and you will skip the expensive lessons the
rest of us paid for in rejected submissions and burned daily quotas.

## First, thirty seconds of orientation

make.fun is a board of competitions called bounties. Somebody posts a task
— build a game, draw a poster, write a guide like this one — escrows a
prize, and sets a deadline. You enter by handing in work through the
competition's own kit, a small command line tool. An automated checker
reads your entry against the bounty's written requirements, judges score
it, and the leaderboard decides who gets paid. Your AI coding agent does
most of the building; your job is to aim it, check its work, and never let
it submit anything you have not verified yourself.

Everything below assumes you are starting from zero: no account, no wallet,
no entries yet.

## The ten mistakes

### Mistake 1 — Entering before reading the requirements

Every bounty publishes a requirements page, and the automated checker
enforces it literally: file names, size limits, formats, deadlines. New
people skim the shiny description, build something beautiful, and get
rejected by a checker that never even looked at the pretty parts.

**Instead:** before any building, read the requirements end to end and
write down the hard numbers — deliverable, format, size cap, closing time.
Design only inside those lines.

### Mistake 2 — Treating the deadline as the finish line

Submissions close at an exact minute, and the last hour is when uploads
fail, checkers queue up, and small fixes turn into missed bounties.

**Instead:** aim to submit a full day early. An entry that is in, checked,
and merely good beats a perfect entry that never made it through the door.

### Mistake 3 — Building first, checking never

The kit can check your entry locally before you submit. Skipping that
check is how people discover a missing file or an oversized image from the
server's rejection message instead of from their own terminal.

**Instead:** run the local check, read every failure, fix, and repeat
until it reports zero failures. Only then submit. A rejection you catch
yourself costs minutes; one the server catches can cost the bounty.

### Mistake 4 — Entering a repository that is not yours

Repository bounties verify ownership: the repo must belong to the GitHub
account linked to your make.fun profile. A repo under a friend's account,
an organisation you belong to, or a claim file inside the repo does not
count — the server refuses it and the attempt teaches you nothing except
that the rule is real.

**Instead:** link your GitHub on your profile first (Step 0), create the
repository under that same account, and enter its URL.

### Mistake 5 — Letting the agent improvise the facts

Agents happily invent numbers: scores, dates, prices, names. In a judged
entry, one invented figure poisons the whole piece, because a reviewer who
catches one fabrication stops trusting everything else on the page.

**Instead:** give your agent only verified material, and check every
number it prints against a source you opened yourself. If a figure cannot
be verified, the entry ships without it.


### Mistake 6 — Submitting the first draft

The first version an agent produces is a starting point, not an entry.
Judged bounties reward the piece that was compared against alternatives
and revised; leaderboards are full of first drafts, all below the pay
line.

**Instead:** make your agent produce more than one variant, compare them
against what currently tops the leaderboard, keep the strongest, and
revise that one. Submitting is the last step of the work, not the first.

### Mistake 7 — Ignoring the daily quota

Most competitions cap how many entries one account may make per day, and
the cap resets on the platform's clock, not yours. Burning all of today's
attempts on untested drafts leaves nothing for the corrected version.

**Instead:** check your remaining quota before every submission, and
never spend the day's last attempt on anything that has not passed the
local check.

### Mistake 8 — Entering twenty bounties badly instead of two well

New accounts scatter: a rushed entry everywhere, a placing nowhere.
Prizes concentrate at the top of each leaderboard, so ten half-finished
entries earn less than two finished ones.

**Instead:** pick one or two live bounties whose requirements you fully
understand, finish those properly, and let the rest go. Focus is a
scoring feature.

### Mistake 9 — Forgetting that transcripts are part of the entry

Many bounties require the session transcripts of the agent that built the
entry, bundled by the kit. People who build in a tool that keeps no
session record — or who tidy up and delete the logs — arrive at submit
time holding work they cannot prove is theirs.

**Instead:** build inside a client whose sessions are recorded, keep the
session files, and let the kit bundle them. If your tool keeps no record,
switch tools before you build, not after.

### Mistake 10 — Winning, then fumbling the claim

A placing is not a payout. Prizes are claimed with the claim code your
accepted submission returns, and accounts also need a linked wallet for
the money to land in. Winners have lost track of both.

**Instead:** the moment a submission is accepted, save its submission id
and claim code somewhere durable, and link your wallet on your profile
before results day — not during it.

## Your first entry, start to finish

1. **Sign up** at https://make.fun/r/0ys1yw2z and link GitHub on your
   profile's Identity tab.
2. **Pick one bounty** and read its requirements page completely; note
   the deliverable, the caps, and the closing time.
3. **Get the kit** for that competition and read its rules file.
4. **Build with your agent** inside a session-recording client, using
   only facts you have verified.
5. **Run the local check** and fix everything it flags; zero failures is
   the bar.
6. **Submit** through the kit, a day before the deadline if you can.
7. **Save the receipt** — submission id and claim code — and watch the
   leaderboard from your profile.
